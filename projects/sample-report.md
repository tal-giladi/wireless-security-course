# Sample wireless engagement report (synthetic lab)

This is a worked model report for a fictional, owned lab environment, built to show what a good capstone write-up looks like end to end. Every network, credential, and artifact here is **synthetic** (`Lab-WiFi-Passw0rd!`, `LAB-FLAG-<uuid>`) and every target is owned lab equipment — nothing in it describes a real network. Follow its structure, which is [`../templates/report-template.md`](../templates/report-template.md), when you write your own capstone report; copy its honesty, not its specific values.

> [!NOTE]
> **LAB TARGET vs REAL NETWORK.** This report documents an assessment of owned lab equipment only, authorised by the lab, using synthetic credentials. In a real engagement the secrets below would be redacted and only authorised, in-scope targets would appear. "Northwind Labs" is fictional.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Artifact checksums read **PENDING** because the lab's shipped captures are generated by a verified run that has not yet happened ([`../labs/common/CAPTURES.md`](../labs/common/CAPTURES.md)). In a submitted report every generated artifact carries a real SHA-256 from your own run; **never invent a checksum**.

## Executive summary

We assessed the four wireless networks of the Northwind Labs test environment over one day, against owned equipment and a signed authorisation. Overall risk is **High**, driven by two issues: the staff network uses a weak, guessable passphrase that we recovered in minutes, and the enterprise network accepts clients that do not validate the RADIUS server certificate, allowing credential capture via a rogue server. The guest network additionally exposed a PMKID and had WPS enabled, widening the attack surface. The secure network resisted assessment as intended — its strong passphrase did not fall and is reported as a control working, not a gap. The three most urgent fixes are: replace the staff passphrase (or move to WPA3-SAE) and enable PMF; enforce server-certificate validation on all enterprise clients; and disable WPS on the guest AP. After remediation we retested and each demonstrated attack failed. Residual risk after the recommended fixes is **Low**, contingent on retiring three legacy clients that cannot do SAE.

## Scope & authorisation

- **In scope (allowlist):** `Northwind-Staff` (BSSID `02:00:00:aa:01:01`), `Northwind-Guest` (`02:00:00:aa:02:01`), `Northwind-Secure` (`02:00:00:aa:03:01`), `Northwind-Corp` (enterprise, `02:00:00:aa:04:01`). All on 2.4/5 GHz lab channels. Everything else was explicitly **out of scope**.
- **Authorised by / window:** Northwind Labs (the equipment owner — in this course, you), for a single-day window; see the filled [`../templates/authorisation-template.md`](../templates/authorisation-template.md).
- **Transmission constraints:** minimum TX power, specific lab channels, RF-attenuated bench; stop conditions per the authorisation record. Transmit-heavy steps preferred on the virtual path.
- **Lab paths used and what each proves:** `Northwind-Staff` and `Northwind-Guest` — **P** (physical RF: real monitor, capture, targeted deauth of our own client). `Northwind-Secure` — **P** (capture) + **R** (offline cracking). `Northwind-Corp` enterprise rogue-server step — **V** (virtual `mac80211_hwsim` + FreeRADIUS), credited as virtual, **not** physical RF.

## Methodology

- **Tools (pinned, [`../TOOLS.md`](../TOOLS.md)):** `hcxdumptool`/`hcxtools`, `aircrack-ng`, `hashcat` (`-m 22000`), `hostapd`/`hostapd-mana`, `wpa_supplicant`, FreeRADIUS, `iw`. Versions recorded in the lab notebook.
- **Recon:** passive scan of the allowlisted BSSIDs; read each network's advertised RSN parameters, PMF capability bit, and WPS state.
- **Capture:** 4-way handshake from our own test client (targeted deauth at minimum power on the owned AP); RSN PMKID from M1 where present; SAE exchange for the secure network; rogue-RADIUS credential capture on the virtual enterprise lab.
- **Analysis:** convert captures to `hc22000`; bounded wordlist (`bounded.txt`) and strong-absent wordlist (`strong-absent.txt`); effort estimate from keyspace × measured H/s for the strong case.
- **Reproducibility:** one lab-notebook entry per finding ([`../templates/lab-notebook-template.md`](../templates/lab-notebook-template.md)), recording path, commands, and the vulnerable-vs-hardened measurement.

## Findings

### F-1 · Weak WPA2-PSK passphrase on `Northwind-Staff` — High

- **Affected configuration:** WPA2-PSK, PMF disabled, 18-character but dictionary-based passphrase `Lab-WiFi-Passw0rd!`.
- **Evidence:** `nw-staff-handshake.pcapng` (complete 4-way handshake) → `nw-staff.hc22000`; recovered with `hashcat -m 22000 nw-staff.hc22000 bounded.txt`. Recovered passphrase (synthetic, would be redacted for a real secret): `Lab-WiFi-Passw0rd!`. Artifact SHA-256: **PENDING** (generated set not yet produced — [`../labs/common/CAPTURES.md`](../labs/common/CAPTURES.md)).
- **Why it works / why it may fail:** the offline check (`PMK = PBKDF2(passphrase, SSID, 4096, 256)`, then recompute the MIC) falls to any passphrase a wordlist reaches; capture required a connecting client we could deauth. A high-entropy passphrase would have produced `Exhausted` (see F-3). Cause is passphrase entropy, not the tool.
- **Impact:** recovering the staff passphrase grants an attacker within radio range full access to the staff network and anything reachable on it. On the flat lab segment that includes the internal file share.

### F-2 · PMKID exposure and WPS enabled on `Northwind-Guest` — Medium

- **Affected configuration:** WPA2-PSK guest SSID, AP includes an RSN PMKID in EAPOL M1, WPS enabled.
- **Evidence:** `nw-guest-pmkid.pcapng` (RSN PMKID in M1) → `nw-guest.hc22000`; clientless recovery with `hashcat -m 22000 nw-guest.hc22000 bounded.txt`. WPS observed enabled in beacons. Artifact SHA-256: **PENDING**.
- **Why it works / why it may fail:** PMKID collection is available **only because** this AP includes the PMKID, and it is **not fully passive** — we associated/probed the AP ([`../lessons/module-06/lesson-03.md`](../lessons/module-06/lesson-03.md)). An AP that suppresses the PMKID denies this path. WPS being enabled adds a separate legacy exposure.
- **Impact:** guest-network compromise, plus — because the guest VLAN's isolation could **not** be confirmed from the captures (see Limitations) — a possible pivot that must be verified separately.

### F-3 · `Northwind-Secure` resisted recovery — Informational (control working)

- **Affected configuration:** WPA2-PSK, PMF enabled, 24-character random passphrase.
- **Evidence:** `nw-secure-handshake.pcapng` (complete handshake) → `nw-secure.hc22000`; `hashcat -m 22000 nw-secure.hc22000 strong-absent.txt` → **`Exhausted`**, no recovery. Effort estimate from `hashcat -b -m 22000` (measured H/s) × keyspace shows a full brute force is infeasible in any realistic window. Artifact SHA-256: **PENDING**.
- **Why it works / why it may fail:** the capture was valid (it produced a hashline), so the non-recovery is about the passphrase's entropy, not a bad capture. This is the **strong-password case — failing to crack is the correct outcome** ([`../lessons/module-06/lesson-04.md`](../lessons/module-06/lesson-04.md)).
- **Impact:** none demonstrated. Reported to show the control is effective and should be kept; this is a pass-by-demonstrating-the-protection, not a gap.

### F-4 · Enterprise clients do not validate the RADIUS server certificate (`Northwind-Corp`) — High

- **Affected configuration:** WPA2-Enterprise (PEAP-MSCHAPv2); test client configured **without** server-certificate validation. Assessed on the **virtual path (V)**.
- **Evidence:** rogue RADIUS (`hostapd-wpe`/FreeRADIUS on `mac80211_hwsim`) captured the MSCHAPv2 challenge/response from the non-validating client; `nw-corp-mschapv2.log` → crackable with the bounded list. Synthetic flag `LAB-FLAG-7f3a9c21-0b4e-4d6a-9e8f-2c1a5b6d4e30`. Artifact SHA-256: **PENDING**. **Path note: virtual radios — credited as virtual, not physical RF.**
- **Why it works / why it may fail:** a client that does not validate the server certificate will authenticate to **any** RADIUS server presenting the expected realm, so a rogue server captures the credential exchange ([`../lessons/module-11/lesson-01.md`](../lessons/module-11/lesson-01.md)). A client that validates the server cert and expects a specific identity **rejects** the rogue server (see Retest). This is credential capture, distinct from relay.
- **Impact:** capture and offline cracking of enterprise user credentials, enabling authenticated access as that user. The single most valuable finding, because it compromises identities rather than one shared key.

## Limitations

- **No physical RF evidence for F-4.** The enterprise rogue-server finding was produced on the virtual `mac80211_hwsim` path and is credited as **virtual, not physical RF**. It demonstrates the protocol mechanic, not real driver/range behaviour.
- **Guest VLAN segmentation not verified.** The captures prove the PMKID exposure but say nothing about whether the guest VLAN is actually isolated from internal systems; that requires a separate wired/segmentation test and is **not** asserted here.
- **Checksums PENDING.** The shipped capture set has not yet been generated by a verified run, so artifact SHA-256 values read PENDING rather than real values; a real engagement report would carry real checksums.
- **No WPS brute force performed.** WPS was observed enabled (F-2) but online WPS brute force is treated as legacy and was not run; its presence is reported as exposure, not exploited.
- **Absence is not safety.** A network or configuration not listed here was either out of scope or not reachable in the window; its absence is not a guarantee it is secure.

## Remediation

Prioritised, concrete, and each with the vulnerable-vs-hardened measurement that confirms it took effect.

1. **F-1 (High):** replace the staff passphrase with a 20+-character random passphrase, or move to **WPA3-SAE**, and **enable PMF**. *Confirm:* the bounded crack of a fresh capture now returns `Exhausted`, and a forged deauth is rejected.
2. **F-4 (High):** enforce **server-certificate validation** and an expected server identity on every enterprise client (managed profiles); prefer **EAP-TLS**. *Confirm:* the hardened client **refuses** the rogue RADIUS and logs why (see Retest).
3. **F-2 (Medium):** **disable WPS** on the guest AP and **suppress the PMKID** where the AP supports it; verify guest-VLAN isolation separately. *Confirm:* WPS absent from beacons; no PMKID in M1; segmentation test passes.
4. **F-3:** no action — **keep** the strong passphrase and PMF; use it as the template for the other networks.

## Retest & residual risk

- **F-1 retest (P):** after setting a 24-char random passphrase and enabling PMF, re-captured and re-ran `hashcat -m 22000 … bounded.txt` → **`Exhausted`**; the targeted deauth was **rejected** (PMF). Before: recovered in minutes; after: not recoverable in scope. Fixed.
- **F-2 retest (P):** after disabling WPS and suppressing the PMKID, beacons no longer advertise WPS and M1 contains **no PMKID** → the clientless path is closed. Fixed; VLAN isolation still to be verified on the wire.
- **F-4 retest (V):** after enforcing server-cert validation, the hardened client **rejected** the rogue RADIUS — the supplicant log shows the server-certificate trust failure and no MSCHAPv2 exchange left the client. Before: credentials captured; after: nothing to capture. Fixed (virtual path).

**Residual risk — Low, with one caveat:** moving `Northwind-Secure`-style hardening everywhere and enforcing EAP-TLS would drop **three legacy clients** that cannot do SAE or managed profiles; those devices must be replaced or formally risk-accepted. Until the guest VLAN isolation is verified on the wire, treat guest-to-internal pivot as an open question, not a closed one.
